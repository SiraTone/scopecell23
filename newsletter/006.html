The Sounding Reader is live

Twice a day the National Weather Service launches weather balloons from about 90 sites across the US. The data they send back is some of the most useful in forecasting, and some of the hardest to read. The Sounding Reader is our free tool for reading it in plain English.

What it does

  • Click anywhere in the US. You get the closest balloon launch. Every site on the map is coloured by how strong its storm setup is, from no storm fuel to extreme.
  • Every line explains itself. Hover any line, dot or shaded area on the hodograph to see what it is. Click it for a full explanation with that site's real numbers: wind shear, low-level spin, storm motion.
  • Or let it walk you through. A guided tour steps through the hodograph one piece at a time.
  • The fuel side, too. The skew-T shows temperature, moisture and the rising air parcel, with CAPE and the other ingredients labelled in words.
  • Go deeper in the Sounding Lab. Parcel tables, wind profiles and full thermodynamic indices for when you want the numbers.
  • Any balloon, any day on record. Pick a past date to look back at a historic event.

Where the data comes from

Every sounding is a real National Weather Service balloon launch, which is public domain, served by the Iowa State Mesonet archive. The analysis runs in your browser. There is no account and nothing to install.

What it is not

  • Not a forecast. One balloon describes one place at one moment. The air can change quickly after launch.
  • Educational. It is built to help you learn to read soundings. For warnings and safety decisions, use the National Weather Service and your local officials.
