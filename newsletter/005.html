CS-TOR and the Nov 12 tornadoes

On Thursday morning CS-TOR, our experimental tornado probability map, put its highest chance in the country over Wilmington, Delaware. That afternoon the National Weather Service confirmed a tornado in New Castle County. Here is what that shows, and what it does not.

The forecast

CS-TOR V0.1 is a tornado probability map built on Nadocast (MIT, by Brian Hempel) with its HRRR weights. The 12Z HRRR run, available around 7 AM EST, gave an 11% chance of a tornado within 25 miles of Wilmington. That was the highest value anywhere in the U.S. The 10%-plus area covered about 870 square miles. The 5%-plus area covered about 9,000.

What happened

  • Delaware. The NWS confirmed a tornado in New Castle County, near the Christiana and Newark area. That is inside the 10%-plus area.
  • New Jersey. A second tornado was confirmed near Chesterfield, in Burlington County. That is inside the 5% to 10% area.

What we are not saying

  • One day is not proof. An 11% forecast should verify about 1 time in 9. One tornado under it is consistent with a good forecast, but calibration takes many days to judge.
  • Survey pending. The NWS had not published the EF rating or track when we wrote this. Tornado locations in the video come from news reports and are approximate.
  • Not an official forecast. CS-TOR is a research product. For tornado warnings and safety decisions, use the National Weather Service and your local officials.

What is next

We will update this once the NWS survey is out, and keep scoring CS-TOR on every convective day, including the misses.
