KyCast

KyCast NEXT 1: a small model that downscales GFS into a Kentucky reflectivity and tornado-chance outlook out to 60 hours (the next 1-3 days). It replaces the earlier 5-day model, which was weak beyond day 2. The live viewer shows the most recent published run and warns when it is out of date.

Open the live viewer

The viewer, showing a past case (GFS cycle 2025-04-01 00Z, valid 2025-04-02). It illustrates the interface, not skill: the case may have been in the training data.

KyCast takes coarse output from NOAA's GFS and produces a 3 km (0.03 degree) grid over Kentucky and its neighbours (35.0-40.5 N, 91.0-80.4 W). The outlook runs 60 hours in 3-hour steps and carries a reflectivity layer and a tornado-chance layer. The viewer shows reflectivity, tornado chance (3-hour or convective-day), the chance of 35+ dBZ and raw GFS for comparison on one synced timeline.

How it was checked

On 1,152 forecast/analysis pairs from 36 whole days the network never saw (19 had tornadoes in the grid), against HRRR analyses and SPC tornado reports. Scores are Brier skill against climatology: above 0 beats always forecasting the base rate.

Honest limits

  • The test was small. Only 19 of the 36 held-out days had tornadoes, so the scores can move a lot with a few more events.
  • We cap the top end. In testing, chances above about 15% ran higher than what actually happened, so a daily tornado chance is never shown above 15%.
  • It is not a sharper storm picture. At day 1, on a 33 km neighbourhood overlap test of 25 dBZ reflectivity (fractions skill score), raw GFS still scored higher (0.46) than thresholded KyCast maps (0.43, at a 25% probability cut). At day 2 they are level (0.34 and 0.35), which on 36 days is a tie. What KyCast adds is calibrated probabilities.
  • Short range only. NEXT 1 was trained for the next 1-2 days and forecasts only to 60 hours. It shows where storms are possible, not which cell will do what.
  • The reflectivity is derived, not simulated. It is built from probabilities, not from a physics model of the atmosphere.
  • Narrow training. It learned from a limited set of days (2021-2026, 1,512 pairs, part of them scored against observed MRMS radar) and one GFS version. We do not know how it behaves in weather patterns it has not seen.
  • No head-to-head with HRRR forecasts yet. We checked against HRRR analyses, but have not compared KyCast with HRRR's own forecasts, so we cannot say how it ranks against them.
  • Experimental. This is not an official forecast. Please do not use it for safety decisions.
