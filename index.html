KY-Net

A small model that downscales GFS into a 5-day Kentucky reflectivity and tornado-chance outlook.

The viewer, showing a past case (GFS cycle 2025-04-01 00Z, valid 2025-04-02). It illustrates the interface, not skill: the case may have been in the training data.

KY-Net takes coarse output from NOAA's GFS and produces a 3 km (0.03 degree) grid over Kentucky and its neighbours (35.0-40.5 N, 91.0-80.4 W). The outlook runs 5 days in 3-hour steps and carries a reflectivity layer and a tornado-chance layer. The viewer shows reflectivity, tornado chance (3-hour or convective-day), the chance of 35+ dBZ and raw GFS for comparison on one synced timeline.

A full 5-day product is a few MB. Once the GFS data is downloaded, running the network for a whole forecast took about 2 seconds; the download dominates.

How it was checked

On 864 forecast/analysis pairs from 36 whole days the network never saw (19 had tornadoes in the grid), against HRRR analyses and SPC tornado reports. Scores are Brier skill against climatology: above 0 beats always forecasting the base rate.

Honest limits

  • Few events: only 19 tornado days in the check. The numbers are noisy.
  • Tornado probabilities above about 15% were overconfident in testing, so a daily chance is never shown above 15%.
  • On neighbourhood overlap (fractions skill score, 33 km) of 25 dBZ reflectivity at day 1, raw GFS scored higher (0.46) than thresholded KY-Net maps (0.25, at a 25% probability cut). KY-Net's advantage is calibrated probabilities, not a sharper storm picture.
  • Reflectivity at days 3-5 is a statistical picture of where storms are possible, not a prediction of individual cells.
  • The reflectivity layer is derived from probabilities, not a physics simulation.
  • Trained on a limited sample of days (2021-2025) and one GFS version; behaviour in other regimes is unknown.
  • It was not compared with HRRR forecasts.
  • Experimental. Not an official forecast. Do not use it for safety decisions.
